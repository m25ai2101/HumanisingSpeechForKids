The Little Fox and the Moonlit Forest

Once upon a time, in a cosy burrow beneath an old oak tree, there lived a little fox named Ember. Every evening she would curl up with her soft, russet tail wrapped around her nose and drift into the most peaceful sleep. The world felt warm and safe, and everything was just as it should be.

One autumn night, Ember crept out of her burrow to play in the moonlit meadow. The silver light danced across the dew-covered grass, and she leapt and tumbled and spun with pure, unbounded joy. She laughed her high little fox laugh and chased fireflies until her paws ached and her heart was so full she thought it might burst.

But then — all at once — the fireflies vanished. The wind dropped completely. The meadow went utterly, horribly silent. Something enormous was moving through the dark trees at the edge of the forest, something that snapped branches and exhaled like thunder. Ember froze. Her ears pressed flat. She could not breathe, could not run, could not make a single sound as the monstrous shadow crept closer and closer through the darkness.

It was only Old Badger, shuffling home from his evening walk. He blinked his small round eyes at Ember and chuckled. She laughed too — a shaky, breathless laugh — and her legs still trembled even though the danger was gone. She felt silly and relieved and a little sad all at once, the way you do after a fright that turned out to be nothing at all.

"You should not be out alone on nights like this," said Old Badger gently, and he walked beside her all the way home. His voice was low and slow and kind, like the sound of rain on dry leaves, and Ember felt her heartbeat settle with every step they took together through the quiet, silver forest.

Back in her burrow, Ember curled up tighter than ever. The adventure had been the most thrilling, most terrifying, most wonderful thing that had ever happened to her. She decided she would do it all again tomorrow night — and the night after that — and every night until she knew every shadow in the forest by name.
